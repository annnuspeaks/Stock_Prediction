# 1.1 Business Requirements

This document defines the business-level requirements for **Nexora ---
AI Stock Decision Support & Prediction Tool**.

> **Scope principle:** Nexora provides decision support, not guaranteed
> financial outcomes or personalized financial advice.

## 1.1 Business Problem

Individual investors, students/researchers, and general users can find
it difficult to convert large amounts of stock-market information into a
structured and understandable decision-support view.

Relevant information may be distributed across price movements, trading
volume, market context, analytical indicators, predictive outputs, and
risk-related signals. Users may therefore need to manually collect,
interpret, and combine multiple pieces of information before forming
their own view of a stock.

Nexora addresses this problem by bringing market information, data
processing, prediction, risk assessment, and recommendation logic into a
single software product.

The system is intended to produce understandable decision-support
outcomes such as **BUY, SELL, WATCH, HOLD, or NO SIGNAL**, while keeping
the user's final decision separate from the system recommendation.

## 1.2 Why the Tool Is Needed

Nexora is needed to provide a structured way of transforming market
information into an understandable analytical workflow.

The tool is intended to:

1.  Reduce the need to manually combine multiple analytical signals.
2.  Present stock analysis in a consistent workflow.
3.  Connect market-data processing with predictive analysis.
4.  Consider risk and confidence instead of presenting a prediction in
    isolation.
5.  Explain the reasoning behind a recommendation in understandable
    language.
6.  Clearly distinguish model prediction, system recommendation, and the
    user's final decision.
7.  Provide a foundation that can be extended with additional models and
    analytical capabilities.
8.  Demonstrate how machine learning can be integrated into a
    maintainable software product.

Nexora is positioned as an **AI-assisted stock decision-support
platform**, not as an automated trading system.

## 1.3 Expected Value

### 1.3.1 User Value

Nexora aims to provide:

-   A single interface for structured stock analysis.
-   Easier interpretation of market and predictive information.
-   Understandable recommendation outputs.
-   Visibility into risk and confidence.
-   Explanations for generated results.
-   Consistent analysis across supported stocks and prediction horizons.
-   Clear handling of insufficient, stale, or unreliable data through
    appropriate states such as **NO SIGNAL**.

### 1.3.2 Product Value

Nexora establishes a reusable platform workflow:

``` text
Market Information
        ↓
Data Processing
        ↓
Prediction
        ↓
Risk Assessment
        ↓
Decision Support
        ↓
User Understanding
```

The platform provides a foundation for additional prediction models,
feature engineering, data providers, validation/backtesting, deployment,
and continuous improvement.

### 1.3.3 Academic and Engineering Value

Nexora demonstrates integration of requirements engineering, Agile
development, Git/GitHub, CI, frontend development, backend/API
development, data processing, machine learning, prediction, risk-aware
decision logic, testing, documentation, and eventual deployment.

The objective is to build a complete and maintainable software product,
not merely an isolated ML model.

## 1.4 Primary Use Cases

### Use Case 1 --- Search for a Stock

The user searches for a supported stock using its symbol or available
stock-search mechanism.

**Expected outcome:** Nexora identifies and presents the relevant stock
for selection.

### Use Case 2 --- Select a Stock

The user selects a stock to analyze.

**Expected outcome:** Nexora loads the selected stock context and makes
it available for analysis.

### Use Case 3 --- Request Stock Analysis

The user requests analysis for the selected stock and chooses the
applicable analysis period or prediction horizon.

**Expected outcome:** Nexora retrieves, validates, and processes the
required market information and initiates prediction.

### Use Case 4 --- Receive Prediction

Nexora applies the selected prediction workflow to prepared data.

**Expected outcome:** The system produces a supported prediction output
and, where applicable, probability/confidence information.

### Use Case 5 --- Assess Risk

Nexora evaluates relevant risk factors such as volatility, data quality,
model confidence, and market conditions where implemented.

**Expected outcome:** The prediction is considered together with its
associated risk and confidence.

### Use Case 6 --- Generate Decision Support

Nexora combines prediction, risk, confidence, and data-quality
considerations.

**Possible outcomes:** BUY, SELL, WATCH, HOLD, or NO SIGNAL.

### Use Case 7 --- Understand the Recommendation

The user reviews the factors and explanation associated with the
generated result.

**Expected outcome:** The user can distinguish the model prediction,
Nexora recommendation, and their own final decision.

### Use Case 8 --- Handle Insufficient or Unreliable Data

If required information is missing, stale, invalid, unavailable, or
insufficient, Nexora should avoid presenting misleading certainty.

**Expected outcome:** An appropriate error, no-data, stale-data, or **NO
SIGNAL** state is presented.

## 1.5 Success Criteria

### Functional Success

-   A user can search for and select a supported stock.
-   A user can request analysis for the selected stock.
-   Required market data can be retrieved and processed.
-   The prediction workflow executes when valid data is available.
-   Risk and confidence can be considered where supported.
-   A decision-support outcome can be generated.
-   Results are presented clearly.
-   The system provides understandable reasoning.
-   Loading, error, stale-data, and no-data states are handled.

### Technical Success

-   Frontend and backend remain separate, maintainable application
    layers.
-   Backend APIs validate inputs and return structured responses.
-   Automated tests appropriately cover implemented functionality.
-   CI validates frontend and backend checks automatically.
-   The project remains reproducible and maintainable.
-   No critical regression is introduced by completed features.
-   Model and preprocessing artifacts can be managed consistently once
    the ML pipeline is implemented.

### Quality Success

-   The system avoids misleading certainty.
-   Prediction and recommendation are clearly distinguished.
-   Risk information is communicated where applicable.
-   **NO SIGNAL** can be returned when evidence is insufficient.
-   The interface remains understandable and usable.
-   The architecture can be extended without unnecessary complexity.

### Responsible-Use Success

-   No guaranteed stock-market profits are claimed.
-   Outputs are not presented as personalized financial advice.
-   Human decision-making remains in the loop.
-   Uncertainty and risk are communicated clearly.

## 1.6 Limitations

### Market and Data Limitations

-   Initial market scope is the **Indian equity market**, focused on
    NSE/BSE-listed stocks.
-   Data availability depends on the selected market-data provider.
-   Providers may impose rate limits, historical-depth limits, freshness
    constraints, authentication requirements, or usage restrictions.
-   Market data can become stale, unavailable, incomplete, or
    temporarily unreliable.
-   Corporate actions and market-calendar effects may require additional
    handling.

### Prediction Limitations

-   Predictions are estimates and are inherently uncertain.
-   Historical performance does not guarantee future results.
-   A machine-learning model may fail to generalize to future market
    conditions.
-   Market regimes can change.
-   Model confidence does not guarantee prediction correctness.
-   Prediction quality depends on input-data quality and availability.
-   No model should be treated as a guaranteed source of profitable
    trading decisions.

### Product-Scope Limitations

The initial version does not aim to provide:

-   Guaranteed investment returns.
-   Personalized financial advice.
-   Automatic trading.
-   Unrestricted coverage of every market.
-   A replacement for professional financial advice.
-   A requirement that users follow one prediction without considering
    risk and context.

### Engineering Limitations

-   The initial product prioritizes simple, reliable architecture over
    unnecessary complexity.
-   Advanced capabilities may be deferred until the core workflow is
    stable.
-   Additional models, sentiment analysis, expanded market coverage,
    deployment automation, and other capabilities may be introduced
    incrementally.

## 1.7 Financial-Risk Considerations

### 1.7.1 Prediction Uncertainty

All predictions should be presented as estimates. Nexora must avoid
language or UI patterns that imply certainty or guaranteed outcomes.

### 1.7.2 Risk Visibility

Where supported, users should be able to understand relevant risk
information, including volatility, model confidence, data quality,
market conditions, and other relevant risk factors.

### 1.7.3 NO SIGNAL

Nexora should support **NO SIGNAL** when evidence is insufficient,
unreliable, stale, contradictory, or below defined confidence/risk
thresholds.

### 1.7.4 Human Decision-Making

Nexora is a decision-support tool. The system recommendation must remain
distinct from the user's final investment decision.

``` text
Model Prediction
       ↓
Risk & Confidence Assessment
       ↓
Nexora Recommendation
       ↓
User's Final Decision
```

### 1.7.5 No Guaranteed Returns

Nexora must never claim or imply guaranteed profits, guaranteed
accuracy, or guaranteed investment outcomes.

### 1.7.6 No Automatic Trading in Initial Scope

Automatic trading is outside the initial product scope. Any future move
toward automated trading requires a separate review covering safety,
authorization, compliance, risk controls, monitoring, failure handling,
and regulatory requirements.

### 1.7.7 Regulatory Considerations

Before real-world financial use, applicable legal and regulatory
requirements should be reviewed for the intended market, users,
deployment model, and functionality.
